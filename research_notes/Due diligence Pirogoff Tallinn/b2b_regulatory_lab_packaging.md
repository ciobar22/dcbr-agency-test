## 1. Who the B2B buyers are in Tallinn/Estonia and how a local producer gets listed

### Takeaway
The supermarket chains (Selver, Rimi, Coop, Maxima, Prisma, Lidl) run published or semi-published supplier-intake routes, and several of them actively want local small producers. Selver says it works with about 200 small producers and wants more (Aug 2026). However, buyers screen hard on supply reliability, capacity and logistics, and many chains also run in-house kitchens that compete directly with pies (Selveri Köök, Coop Kokad). Café chains are a mixed prospect. Reval Café (13 cafés) has a sister confectionery company, Reval Kondiiter, so third-party pastry buying is probably limited. Caffeine belongs to Norway's Reitan group (which also owns R-Kiosk), so listing decisions there probably sit at group level. Coffee People and Gourmet Coffee are small specialty operators controlled by the same person, Annar Alas.

## 2. Typical wholesale margins/markups and delivery models

### Takeaway
No bakery-specific margin data for Estonia was found. At the aggregate level, Estonian food retailers' markup on wholesale price was reported at about 26% (Eesti Pank analysis, contested by retailers). The Konjunktuuriinstituut found that the retail margin rose to about 41–45% of the retail price in 2020–21. Delivery models for pies in Estonia include daily fresh delivery and frozen bake-off (Eesti Pagar, Circle K in-store baking, Horeca Service distribution).

### Cited Findings
- **Eesti Pank:** the average retail markup on food is now about 26% added to wholesale prices, against 18% ten years earlier. Retailers dispute this. — [ERR](https://www.err.ee/1609823976/eesti-panga-hinnangul-on-juurdehindlus-selgelt-kasvanud-kaupmehed-ei-noustu)
- **Konjunktuuriinstituut (Economic Research Institute), food price margin study:** the retail margin share of the retail price rose to 45% by end-2020 and fell to 41% in Q2 2021. The same summary also gives the producer price share as 60% in H1 2021. These two figures are inconsistent if taken together, so read the PDF before quoting either. — [Konjunktuuriinstituut via pikk.ee](https://www.pikk.ee/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Toidukaupade_hinnamarginaalid_2021_II.pdf)
- **Frozen pirukad in retail packs** (Eesti Pagar) and frozen bakery distribution to HoReCa (Horeca Service) exist. — [Eesti Pagar](https://eestipagar.ee/tooted/sugavkulmutatud-tooted/jaepakendid/); [Horeca Service](https://horecaservice.ee/index.php/products/group/34/K%C3%BClmutatud+pagaritooted)
- **Circle K bakes rolls daily in its stations**, a bake-off model. — [Circle K](https://www.circlek.ee/toiduvalik)
- **Payment terms for perishable food are capped at 30 days** (see section 1). — [EPKK](https://epkk.ee/maksetahtaeg-toiduainete-tarnimisel-ei-tohiks-uletada-30-paeva/)

### Inferences
*These are industry rules of thumb, not sourced for Estonia. Validate them in buyer conversations.*
- **Supermarket margin:** the retailer typically wants a 25–40% gross margin on short-shelf-life bakery items. Write-off (unsold product) is either borne by the supplier through returns, or priced into a higher margin. Clarify returns/"tagastus" terms in every contract.
- **Café markup:** cafés typically price pastries at 2.5–3.5× the purchase price before VAT, consistent with a 60–70% gross margin target. A pie wholesaled at about €1.20–1.80 would then retail at about €3.50–5.50. These are illustrative numbers, not sourced.
- **Daily fresh delivery** (early morning, chilled van) fits cafés and offices within Tallinn and near Viimsi. It requires a short-route delivery van and staff working before 7:00.
- **Frozen par-baked or fully baked products for thaw-and-serve or bake-off** fit fuel stations, convenience stores and chains outside Tallinn. They need blast freezing, frozen storage and a freezer distribution partner, and allow weekly deliveries.

### Gaps
- No Estonia-specific bakery wholesale or retail markups, no café chain purchase prices for pastries, and no Selver/Rimi margin terms were found.
- Typical return and write-off terms for fresh bakery in Estonian chains were not found.

## 3. Regulatory path in Estonia (PTA registration vs. approval, HACCP, labelling, micro criteria, cold chain)

### Takeaway
Since the Food Act amendment effective 1 April (2021), Estonia requires a PTA operating licence (tegevusluba) only where EU law requires approval, chiefly Reg. 853/2004 establishments handling products of animal origin, plus national cases such as school and hospital catering. A bakery making pies from plant ingredients, or from processed animal ingredients bought from approved plants (composite products), only needs to submit a business activity notice. A licence becomes necessary if Pirogoff itself processes raw meat or fish into fillings and supplies them to other businesses beyond "marginal, local and restricted" limits: 100 kg per week or 35% of weekly animal-food output, within 300 km. All products must carry Estonian-language labels compliant with Reg. 1169/2011, including the nutrition declaration (which can be calculated). From 1 July 2026, ready-to-eat foods face the stricter Listeria rules of Reg. 2024/2895.

### Cited Findings
**Licence vs. notification**
- **Food Act amendment, in force from 1 April:** operating licences are now required only in sectors specified by directly applicable EU regulations, plus national licences for catering in pre-schools, schools, healthcare/social care and the military. Other businesses submit a business activity notice (majandustegevusteade). Notification replaced licensing in non-animal primary product handling, non-animal food and compound food handling, and some animal-product handling. The amending act was published in Riigi Teataja on 23.02.2021, which implies 1 April 2021. — [agri.ee](https://www.agri.ee/uudised/homsest-joustuv-toiduseaduse-muudatus-lihtsustab-toidukaitlejate-tegevust); [EPKK](https://epkk.ee/1-aprillist-joustuv-toiduseaduse-muudatus-lihtsustab-toidukaitlejate-tegevust/); [Bioneer](https://bioneer.ee/toiduseaduse-muudatus-toob-leevendust-toiduk%C3%A4itlejatele); [RT I, 23.02.2021, 2](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/123022021002)
- **Animal-origin food supplied to another retail business** needs only notification if the activity is marginal, local and restricted. Otherwise a licence is required.
  - "Marginal" means at most 100 kg per week, or at most 35% of the weekly total of processed animal food.
  - "Local" means delivery within Estonia, at most 300 km from the production site, to the operator's own or another retail business.
  - Source: [PTA: home food for sale](https://pta.agri.ee/tarbijale-ja-eraisikule/toit/kodus-toidu-valmistamine-muugiks); [PTA: Toidu müük](https://pta.agri.ee/toidu-muuk) (snippets)
- **The list of handling fields and food groups** that determines whether a notice or a licence is needed is in the Ministry regulation (MM 15.09.2023 No. 59, annex 1). — [Riigi Teataja annex](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/aktilisa/1160/9202/3006/MM_15092023_m59_lisa1.pdf)
- **Home kitchens:** preparing food in a private home under a notification applies only when the food is not supplied to retail businesses or other food handlers. B2B supply therefore needs commercial premises or a licence or notice that covers it. — [PTA: Kodus toidu valmistamine müügiks](https://pta.agri.ee/tarbijale-ja-eraisikule/toit/kodus-toidu-valmistamine-muugiks)
- **Changes to the data in a notice or licence** must be reported to PTA within 5 working days. — [PTA: tegevusload ja teavitamine](https://pta.agri.ee/ettevotjale-tootjale-ja-turustajale/toidu-tootmine/tegevusload-ja-teavitamine) (snippet)
- **Composite products under EU law** (EC guidance on Reg. 853/2004, rev. 2023):
  - Composite products are foods containing both plant products and processed products of animal origin. They are not "products of animal origin".
  - An establishment handling only composite products does not need 853/2004 approval.
  - However, the processed animal ingredients must come from approved establishments.
  - Adding a plant ingredient to a processed animal product does not automatically make it a composite product.
  - Sources: [EC guidance SANCO/10098/2009 rev. 2023](https://food.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2020-05/biosafety_fh_legis_guidance_reg-2004-853_en.pdf); [EC composite products Q&A](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/caef7035-91fc-491a-a0b2-b40dad2ec43b_en?filename=ia_ic_composite-prods_qandas.pdf)
- **UK FSA approvals policy (comparator):** meat pies and sausage rolls are given as examples of animal-origin products whose manufacture can require approval. — [FSA approvals policy](https://www.food.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/document/meat-estab-approvals-policy-v4-final.pdf)

**Self-monitoring and labelling**
- **PTA publishes a template self-monitoring plan** ("Ettevõtte enesekontrolli plaan") and a guide to compiling one (HACCP-based, Reg. 852/2004). — [PTA plan template](https://pta.agri.ee/media/2772/download); [PTA guide](https://pta.agri.ee/media/2420/download)
- **Estonian language:** Food Act §38 requires food that is sold or otherwise transferred to be labelled in Estonian, so that both the next handler and the consumer get the necessary information. The detailed rules are in the government regulation on food labelling requirements. — [Toiduseadus (Riigi Teataja)](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/117112021020); [Labelling regulation](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/688026); [Toiduteave: mandatory particulars](https://toiduteave.ee/margistamine/kohustuslikud-andmed/); [PTA labelling presentation 23.11.2022](https://toiduteave.ee/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Margistamise_Infopaev_23.11.22_Hellika_Kallaste.pdf)
- **Nutrition declaration:**
  - Values may come from the producer's analysis, from generally established and accepted data, or from calculation based on known or actual average ingredient values.
  - In Estonia, the NutriData calculation and entry program of the Health Development Institute (TAI) is an accepted data source.
  - Salt = sodium × 2.5. Energy is stated in kJ first, then kcal, per 100 g.
  - Sources: [PTA: toitumisalane teave](https://pta.agri.ee/toitumisalane-teave); [Toiduteave: toitumisalane teave](https://toiduteave.ee/margistamine/toitumisalane-teave/); [NutriData presentation (16.06.2021)](https://toiduteave.ee/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Esitlus_Ann_Jogi_NutriData_16.06.21.pdf); [Nutridata.ee](https://www.nutridata.ee/et/)

**Microbiological criteria (Listeria)**
- **Reg. 2073/2005:** a ready-to-eat food counts as not supporting Listeria growth if pH ≤ 4.4, or aw ≤ 0.92, or pH ≤ 5.0 together with aw ≤ 0.94, or its shelf life is under 5 days. For such foods the limit is 100 cfu/g throughout shelf life. — [Reg. 2073/2005 (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2005/2073/oj/eng); [EC Listeria guidance](https://food.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-10/biosafety_fh_mc_guidance_document_lysteria.pdf)
- **Reg. (EU) 2024/2895, applicable from 1 July 2026:** for ready-to-eat foods that support growth, "not detected in 25 g" now applies throughout shelf life (also after the product leaves the producer's control), unless the producer can demonstrate that the level will stay at or below 100 cfu/g. — [Reg. 2024/2895 (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202402895); [FSAI](https://www.fsai.ie/news-and-alerts/latest-news/new-european-commission-guidance-issued-to-support)
- **PTA guidance** "Toidu säilimisaja määramine" (parts I–III, 2017/2019/2020) covers durability tests, challenge tests (nakatamiskatse) and predictive models. Producers of ready-to-eat foods in which Listeria can grow must show that L. monocytogenes stays at or below 100 cfu/g throughout shelf life. Part III gives storage requirements by food group. — [PTA guide part I](https://pta.agri.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2021-01/Toidu_sailimisaja_maaramise_juhend_I_osa_korrigeeritud_variant_2019_detsember.pdf); [Part II](https://toiduteave.ee/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Toidu_s%C3%A4ilimisaja_m%C3%A4%C3%A4ramise_juhend_II-osa_2020.pdf); [Part III (docplayer copy)](https://docplayer.ee/203532229-Toidu-säilimisaja-määramine-iii-osa-toidu-säilitamisnõuded-toidugruppide-kaupa.html); [PTA shelf-life slides (2024)](https://kalateave.ee/images/2024/teabematerjalid/Sailimisaeg.pdf); [PTA Listeria](https://pta.agri.ee/listeria); [Listeria info sheet 2022](https://toiduteave.ee/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Listeria_monocytogenes_toidupatogeenina_infoleht_veebi_2022.pdf)
- **Estonia also has a national regulation on microbiological requirements by food group.** Its current validity was not checked. — [Riigi Teataja akt 72021](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/72021)

### Inferences
- **Registration vs. licence for Pirogoff:**
  - If fillings are made in-house from raw minced meat or raw fish, and pies go to many B2B customers, volumes will soon exceed 100 kg per week. Pirogoff would then probably need a PTA operating licence (tegevusluba) as an approved establishment (Reg. 853/2004).
  - The alternative is to buy cooked meat or fish fillings, or processed animal ingredients, from approved suppliers and make only composite products. Then a notice may be enough.
  - Cabbage, potato, apple and berry pies are plant-based. Pies with tvorog (cottage cheese) and egg use processed dairy and egg bought from approved plants, which supports the composite route.
  - **Confirm the classification with PTA's Harju regional office before scaling.** Approval involves a pre-licensing inspection of the premises.
- **Self-monitoring plan:** a B2B supplier's plan must add hazards and controls for transport temperature, traceability (lot codes on labels), allergen cross-contact, a recall procedure, and shelf-life validation records.
- **Labels for retail sale need, at minimum** (from the content of Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 9; unsourced in this session): name, ingredients in descending order with allergens emphasised, quantity of characterising ingredients (QUID, e.g. "meat 25%") where applicable, net quantity, a best-before or use-by date, storage conditions, the business name and address, a nutrition declaration, and a lot number. All of it in Estonian; Russian or English can be added.
  - Fresh pies supplied unpacked to cafés count as "non-prepacked". The café must still be able to give the consumer allergen information, so the supplier must send allergen data with each delivery.
- **Use-by vs. best-before:** chilled pies with meat, fish or tvorog fillings that support microbial growth would normally carry "kõlblik kuni" (use-by). Ambient dry or sweet items would carry "parim enne" (best-before). This is an inference; PTA guide part I explains the choice.
- **Transport:** Reg. 852/2004 Annex II, Ch. IV requires clean, maintained conveyances that can keep foods at the right temperature and allow temperature to be monitored. Estonian chilled storage is commonly specified as +2…+6 °C for perishable ready-to-eat foods (see PTA guide part III). This is from general knowledge and was not verified in this session.

- PTA's official fee (riigilõiv) and processing time for an operating licence or a notice were not found. From memory, a notice is free and processed via the PTA self-service portal, and a licence decision takes up to 30 working days; neither was verified.
- The exact text of the PTA notice/licence decision matrix for "bakery products with meat filling" could not be read (pta.agri.ee blocked).
- Specific Estonian vehicle registration or approval requirements for food transport were not researched.

## 5. Shelf-life technology for yeast-dough pies (chilled, ambient, frozen; MAP; water activity)

### Takeaway
Shelf stability at ambient temperature generally requires low water activity (fillings aw ≤ about 0.85 and/or pH < 4.6 with heat treatment). Moist savory fillings (meat, fish, egg, cabbage, potato) and tvorog fillings sit well above that, so they cannot safely be ambient beyond same-day sale. They need chilling, or freezing for longer distribution. High-CO2 modified-atmosphere packaging (≥60% CO2) can extend the mould-free life of bakery goods from days to weeks, but it does not make high-aw meat or fish fillings safe at ambient temperature. A "no-fridge" product is realistic only for low-aw sweet lines (for example jam or cooked-fruit fillings formulated to aw ≤ about 0.85) or dry baked goods.

### Cited Findings
- **Shelf-stable fillings** generally require pH below 4.6 and aw ≤ 0.85, combined with pasteurisation or heat processing. — [SupplySide SJ: "Fillings: What's Inside Counts"](https://www.supplysidesj.com/specialty-nutrients/fillings-whats-inside-counts)
- **Water-activity thresholds:** moulds and yeasts can grow from about aw 0.80–0.90, and above aw 0.85 bacterial, yeast and mould spoilage becomes a significant concern for bakery products. — [Smith et al., "Shelf Life and Safety Concerns of Bakery Products – A Review"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8624535_Shelf_Life_and_Safety_Concerns_of_Bakery_Products_-_A_Review); [Novatron app note](https://www.novatron.co.uk/application-notes/water-activity-in-bakery-products/)
- **High-CO2 MAP:** packing bakery goods in high CO2 can extend mould-free shelf life "from days to weeks and months". — [Campden BRI blog](https://www.campdenbri.co.uk/blogs/modified-atmosphere-packing.php)
- **In one study, 100% CO2 prevented fungal growth for 28 days at 25 °C regardless of aw.** In other work, 70% CO2 / 30% N2 gave the best results. Bakery MAP typically uses ≥60% CO2 with N2 as filler. — [MAP for bakery products at different pH/aw (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231586852_Modified_Atmosphere_Packaging_for_Prevention_of_Mold_Spoilage_of_Bakery_Products_with_Different_pH_and_Water_Activity_Levels); [Witt Gas whitepaper](https://www.wittgas.com/fileadmin/user_upload/Dateien/whitepapers/Whitepaper_modified_atmosphere_packaging_US.pdf)
- **Cakes at aw 0.80–0.90** in MAP with oxygen absorbers had significantly longer mould-free life at 25 °C. At higher aw, oxygen absorbers became necessary. — [Active packaging study (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249481534_Mold-free_Shelflife_Extension_of_Bakery_Products_by_Active_Packaging)
- **Moisture migration:** a filling should have an aw similar to or lower than the dough, so water does not move into the crust (sogginess). — [US patent 6753026: long-shelf-life filled bread snacks](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6753026)
- **Listeria non-growth thresholds** (aw ≤ 0.92, pH ≤ 4.4, or shelf life < 5 days) define when a chilled ready-to-eat product escapes the strictest Listeria testing. — [Reg. 2073/2005](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2005/2073/oj/eng); [Chilled Food Association: shelf life of RTE foods vs. Lm](https://www.chilledfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Shelf-life-of-RTE-foods-in-relation-to-Lm-FINAL-v1.1.1-23-3-10-with-worked-examples.pdf)
- **B. cereus**, relevant to rice, potato and egg fillings, does not grow below pH 4.5 or aw 0.91. — [Wikipedia: Bacillus cereus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_cereus) (secondary source; confirm in PTA guide part II)
- **Frozen pirukad** (meat, ham-cheese, bacon-egg) are sold commercially in Estonia (Eesti Pagar). — [Eesti Pagar](https://eestipagar.ee/tooted/sugavkulmutatud-tooted/jaepakendid/)

### Inferences
*Indicative planning ranges, not lab-validated. Each SKU must be validated with a durability study (section 4).*

| Filling | Ambient (≤ 20 °C) | Chilled (0…+4/+6 °C), plain bag/box | Chilled + MAP | Frozen (−18 °C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meat, chicken, fish, egg-rice | Same day only (sell within hours; not recommended for B2B ambient display) | 2–4 days | 5–10 days, *only* with Listeria validation (challenge test if ≥ 5 days) | 3–6 months |
| Cabbage, potato, mushroom | Same day to 1 day | 3–4 days | 5–10 days | 3–6 months |
| Tvorog (cottage cheese) | Same day | 2–4 days | 5–7 days | 2–4 months (texture risk) |
| Apple, berry (fresh fruit) | 1–2 days (mould and sogginess limit) | 3–5 days | 7–14 days | 4–6 months |
| Jam or cooked fruit reduced to aw ≤ 0.85 | 5–10+ days (mould-limited; longer with MAP or preservative) | Not needed | 2–4+ weeks | Not needed |

- **Fillings that cannot safely be ambient** beyond same-day service (their aw and pH sit far above the shelf-stable thresholds): meat, fish, egg, tvorog/cheese, and cooked potato or cabbage.
- **A "no-fridge" B2B product is feasible only for a reformulated sweet line:**
  - high-sugar cooked fruit or jam filling at aw ≤ about 0.85 with pH < 4.6;
  - a drier enriched dough;
  - optionally a clean-label mould inhibitor (cultured wheat or vinegar) or permitted sorbate/propionate (E200–E203, E280–E283) in the dough;
  - MAP or a sealed pack.

  Expect best-before dates of days to a few weeks, not months.
- **For savory lines, the realistic B2B formats are:**
  - daily fresh chilled delivery with a 2–3 day use-by, which keeps shelf life under 5 days (the automatic Listeria non-growth category) and avoids a challenge test;
  - frozen, fully baked thaw-and-serve or par-baked bake-off pies for fuel stations and chains, with a frozen shelf life of months and a stated "after thawing use within X h/days" (needs its own study).
- **MAP needs a tray sealer with gas flushing.** Windowed paperboard boxes are not gas-tight, so MAP and the "bakery box with window" are different packaging formats.

### Gaps
- No Estonian or EU source was found stating validated shelf lives specifically for pirog/pirukad by filling. The table above is an expert estimate and must be validated.
- Campden BRI and IFST shelf-life guides could not be accessed (blocked); their specific recommendations are not reproduced.
- Preservative limits (Reg. 1333/2008) for fine bakery wares were not looked up in this session.

- **A standard B2B spec pack per SKU (industry norm, unsourced) contains:**

  - recipe and ingredient list in descending order, with QUID for characterising ingredients;
  - an allergen matrix covering the 14 EU allergens, including "may contain" cross-contact;

  - PTA registration or approval number;
  - product liability insurance certificate.
- **Phase 1** (cafés, Selver, Coop): PTA approval or notice, a self-monitoring plan, HACCP training records, lab results, and spec sheets.
- **Phase 2** (Rimi/Maxima national listing, fuel-station chains, any private label, Lidl): plan for FSSC 22000 or BRCGS (often 12–18 months of preparation). Small sites could consider BRCGS "START!" or IFS Global Markets as stepping stones; both are unsourced suggestions.

## 7. Packaging suppliers (Estonia/Baltics) and EU packaging rules (PPWR, SUP)

### Takeaway
Several Estonian suppliers sell stock windowed cake and bakery boxes and offer custom printing: Pakendikeskus, EcoWay, Infigo, Multipack, Gemoss and the online printer Print24. Small-run prices were not public. The new EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, Reg. 2025/40) has applied since 12 August 2026 with no general SME exemption. Pirogoff, as a packer and brand owner, should obtain declarations of conformity and technical documentation from its box supplier and prepare for recyclability grading (A–C) by 2030. Mono-material paper boxes, or easily separable windows, reduce that risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Pakendikeskus:** "Estonia's largest selection" of packaging materials and equipment. It sells cake boxes, cake boards and food boxes, and prints on packaging and makes custom packaging. — [Pakendikeskus: koogikarbid](https://www.pakendikeskus.ee/c/toiduainete-pakendid/koogikarbid-koogialused-toidukarbid)
- **EcoWay:** B2B packaging and printing for HoReCa, bakers, confectioners, food producers and café/restaurant chains, with "stable bulk supply". — [EcoWay](https://ecoway.ee/)
- **Infigo:** printed packaging, food boxes and bowls. — [Infigo](https://infigo.ee/tootekategooria/toiduainete-pakendid/)
- **Print24:** online custom-printed food packaging in many sizes. — [Print24 EE](https://print24.com/ee/pakendid/toidu-pakend)
- **Multipack.ee and Gemoss:** stock take-away and salad boxes and lids. — [Multipack](https://multipack.ee/shop/et/toiduainepakendid/take-away-ja-sushi-pakendid/kiirtoidu-ja-salatikarbid); [Gemoss](https://shop.gemoss.ee/et/noud-klaasid-ja-soogiriistad/uhekordsed-lauanoud-ja-pakendid/karbid-ja-kaaned)
- **PPWR timing:** in force 11 Feb 2025 and applicable from 12 Aug 2026, replacing Directive 94/62/EC. There is no general SME exemption.
  - From 12 Aug 2026, packaging must be supported by an EU Declaration of Conformity and technical documentation.
  - Core obligations cover minimisation, recyclability, EPR (extended producer responsibility) registration, and a 40% maximum empty-space ratio for e-commerce packaging.
  - By 1 Jan 2030, packaging must be designed for recycling and meet recyclability grade A, B or C.
  - Sources: [Tanso: PPWR explained](https://www.tanso.de/en/blog/ppwr-eu-packaging-regulation-explained); [DPP-tool PPWR guide](https://dpp-tool.com/en/guide/ppwr/); [Keller & Heckman](https://www.khlaw.com/insights/new-eu-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-highlights-and-challenges-ahead)
  - These are secondary sources (consultancies). The primary text is Reg. (EU) 2025/40 on EUR-Lex.

### Inferences
- **Windowed boxes:** a paperboard box with a glued PET/PLA window is a composite. Under future PPWR recyclability criteria, a plastic window may lower the grade. Alternatives:
  - a die-cut window with no film;
  - a cellulose-film window;
  - a fully paper box with a printed image of the product.

  Ask the supplier for its PPWR DoC and recyclability assessment. This is an inference; check the delegated acts as they are published.
- **Single-Use Plastics Directive (2019/904):** windowed paperboard pastry boxes are generally not "food containers" in its sense. Plastic-lined cups, plastic clamshells or trays for immediate consumption could be, which triggers Estonian SUP measures. Prefer paper-based, plastic-free formats for cafés. ## 8. Public support available 2025–2026

### Takeaway
The best-fitting instruments for Pirogoff are:
- PRIA's food-industry investment support. The 2026 round, open 16–30 Sept 2026, has a €7.4M budget (half for micro/small firms) at 15–50% aid intensity; it can pay for ovens, blast freezers and packaging lines.
- EIS's innovation and development vouchers (up to €7,500 and €35,000), usable for shelf-life studies and reformulation with TFTAK.
- The EIS start-up grant (up to €20k), but only if Pirogoff's company is under 3 years old with sales under €40k.
- Tallinn's small-business digital-solutions grant (up to €6,000 a year), usable for ordering, ERP or EDI.

### Cited Findings
- **PRIA "Toiduainetööstuse investeeringutoetus" 2026:**
  - Round open 16–30 Sept 2026, budget €7.4M: €3.7M for micro and small firms, €3.7M for medium and large.
  - Aid rate 15–50% of eligible costs, depending on firm size and the state-aid basis. Maximum €3.5M per applicant over the CAP strategy period.
  - Applicants must have food or beverage production as their main activity. For micro and small firms, eligible activities include starting or expanding processing via construction or equipment.
  - Minimum score 12 points for micro and small firms. Apply via e-PRIA. Projects must finish within 2 years of the decision and by 30 June 2029 at the latest.
  - Sources: [Tööstusuudised, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.toostusuudised.ee/uudised/2026/09/16/toidutoostustele-avaneb-74-miljoni-eurone-investeeringutoetuse-voor); [PRIA 2025 round page](https://www.pria.ee/toetused/toiduainetoostuse-investeeringutoetus-2025); [Regulation (RT)](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/128062025009); [PRIA info day slides 18.06.2025](https://www.pria.ee/sites/default/files/2025-07/toiduainetoostuse-investeeringutoetuse-infopaev-18.06.2025.pdf)
- **PRIA short supply chains and local markets support, 2026:**
  - Applications ran 22 Apr–6 May 2026.
  - Applicants must be a cooperation of at least two parties, for projects up to 3 years.
  - Entrepreneur partners need at least 2 years of agricultural activity and at least €4,000 in agricultural product sales. Only new cooperations or new activities qualify.
  - Sources: [PRIA](https://www.pria.ee/toetused/luhikeste-tarneahelate-ja-kohalike-turgude-arendamise-toetus-2026); [Regulation No. 73 of 19.08.2025](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/122082025004)

  - Up to €20,000 for companies no more than 3 years old, with fewer than 10 FTE and sales under €40,000.

### Inferences
- **Sequencing:**
  1. Use an EIS innovation voucher (up to €7.5k) for TFTAK or BioCC shelf-life and packaging trials, and for reformulating a shelf-stable sweet line.
  2. Prepare a PRIA food-industry investment application for the next round (likely autumn 2027, as the 2026 round closed 30 Sept 2026) for a blast freezer, a deck/rack oven, a tray sealer and a cold room.
  3. Use the Tallinn digital grant for B2B ordering, ERP or EDI (Telema) integration.
- **The short-supply-chain measure** needs agricultural-producer partners, so it is a poor fit unless Pirogoff partners with farms (e.g. for local berries or tvorog).
