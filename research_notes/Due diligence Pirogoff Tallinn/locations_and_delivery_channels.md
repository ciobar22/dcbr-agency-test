## Q1. Balti Jaama Turg (Baltic Station Market): footfall, tenant mix, leasing, rents, hours, neighbourhood, transport, students

### Takeaway
The landlord says Balti Jaama Turg gets 5.1m+ visits a year and has Tallinn's largest street-food area (40+ stalls/restaurants, up to 300 vendors in season). It sits on Estonia's main railway station (Elron carried 7.66m passengers in 2025, with 20k+ a day) and next to Telliskivi (1.5m+ visitors a year). Short-term micro-stalls (about 1.2 × 1.2 m) can be booked by e-mail with the landlord, Astri. No published rental rates were found, so a quote has to be requested.

### Cited Findings
**Footfall and tenant mix**
- Balti Jaama Turg receives "over 5.1 million visits per year" (landlord figure, year not stated) — [Astri Grupp – BJT leasing page](https://astri.ee/grupp/en/astri-keskkonnad/tule-kauplema/bjt/)
- Up to 300 vendors depending on season; described as "the biggest street food area in Tallinn with over 40 street food stalls and restaurants". The rest of the market covers local raw produce, design, vintage, antiques and handicrafts — [Balti Jaama Turg (Astri) home](https://astri.ee/bjt/en/avaleht/); [Astri Grupp BJT page](https://astri.ee/grupp/en/astri-keskkonnad/tule-kauplema/bjt/) (search-summary attribution)
- Street-food tenants named in listings include Baojaam and Eugenio's Streetfood — [TripAdvisor – Baojaam](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g274958-d12528461-Reviews-Baojaam-Tallinn_Harju_County.html); [TripAdvisor – Eugenio's](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g274958-d14772922-Reviews-Eugenio_s_streetfood-Tallinn_Harju_County.html)
- Nearby pie competitor: Kalamaja Pagarikoda (reviewed as "Pirukas and More") — [TripAdvisor review](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowUserReviews-g274958-d7142113-r758875399-Kalamaja_Pagarikoda-Tallinn_Harju_County.html)

**Owner / operator and history**
- Astri Kinnisvara OÜ (Astri Group) bought Balti Jaama Turg in 2014. Construction of the modern market building began in January 2016 and kept as much of the historic stone buildings as possible — [BJT – Ajalugu (history)](https://astri.ee/bjt/ajalugu/)
- The same group (Astri) also runs Fama Keskus (Narva) and has a similar micro-space scheme there — [Astri Zendesk – Mikropinnad Fama Keskuses](https://astri.zendesk.com/hc/et/articles/115000968589-Mikropinnad-Fama-Keskuses)

**How to become a tenant (short-term micro-spaces)**
- Indoor stalls are offered for short periods. Apply by contacting the market administration or e-mailing **baltijaamaturg@astri.ee** with: company/sole-trader (FIE) name, registry code, contact e-mail and phone, and a list of products — [Astri Zendesk – Mikropinnad Balti Jaama Turul](https://astri.zendesk.com/hc/et/articles/115003293485-Mikropinnad-Balti-Jaama-Turul)
- A typical micro-space is a 1.2 m × 1.2 m table. The market provides sales counters, but the trader brings all other equipment. Locations are allocated from what is free, and the trader's wishes are considered "when possible" — [same source](https://astri.zendesk.com/hc/et/articles/115003293485-Mikropinnad-Balti-Jaama-Turul)
- Short-term renters (1–30 days) must hold a sales ticket issued by the market. Industrial goods (antiques, crafts, souvenirs) are mostly on the 2nd floor. Seasonal produce, flowers and seedlings are sold outdoors on the "Morning" and "Evening" squares — [same source](https://astri.zendesk.com/hc/et/articles/115003293485-Mikropinnad-Balti-Jaama-Turul)
- Astri has a separate help article on events at Balti Jaama Turg (a lead for event/pop-up participation; content not retrieved) — [Astri Zendesk – Üritused Balti Jaama Turul](https://astri.zendesk.com/hc/et/articles/115003743969-%C3%9Critused-Balti-Jaama-Turul)

**Transport traffic at Balti jaam**
- Balti jaam is Estonia's largest railway station; all commuter, long-distance and international trains depart from it — [Wikipedia – Tallinn Baltic Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn_Baltic_Station)
- About 140 passenger trains depart from Balti jaam daily. Elron runs 200+ departures a day and serves over 20,000 passengers daily (network-wide, year not stated) — [et.wikipedia – Balti jaam](https://et.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balti_jaam) (search-summary attribution; verify)
- Elron carried **7.66 million passengers in 2025** (whole network) — [ERR News – New Riga rail link boosts Elron's Baltic passenger numbers](https://news.err.ee/1610100877/new-riga-rail-link-boosts-elron-s-baltic-passenger-numbers)
- About 19,000 people travelled with Elron between the Baltic states over half a year after the new Riga link opened (2026, per ERR) — [ERR – Poole aasta jooksul reisis Elroniga Baltimaade vahel 19 000 inimest](https://www.err.ee/1610100841/poole-aasta-jooksul-reisis-elroniga-baltimaade-vahel-19-000-inimest)
- One July (year probably 2024, from the article ID; verify) had more than 572,000 rail passengers. The busiest lines, all running out of Balti jaam, were Tallinn–Turba (92k), Tallinn–Paldiski (85k) and Tallinn–Aegviidu (74k) — [ERR – Juulis reisis rongiga üle 572 000 reisija](https://www.err.ee/1609414780/juulis-reisis-rongiga-ule-572-000-reisija)
- Tallinn city public transport (TLT): ~133 million passengers in 2023 (buses 103m, trolleybuses 10m, trams 20m). The 2017–2019 average was 142m a year, with 91m in 2020, 76m in 2021 and 96.3m in 2022 — [TLT – nearly 133 million passengers](https://tlt.ee/en/tallinn-public-transport-serviced-nearly-133-million-passengers-last-year/); [ERR – PT use still below pre-pandemic](https://news.err.ee/1609255434/public-transport-use-in-tallinn-still-lower-than-pre-pandemic-levels)
- 2024: "nearly 130 million passengers" used Tallinn public transport — [Tallinn.ee news](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/nearly-130-million-passengers-used-tallinns-public-transport-2024)
- Trams carry roughly 19–20 million journeys a year — [TLT 2023 figures](https://tlt.ee/en/tallinn-public-transport-serviced-nearly-133-million-passengers-last-year/). Since 1 Aug 2026 tram line numbers carry a "T" prefix, and the network has six lines and 45 stops — [tallinntram.com (unofficial guide)](https://tallinntram.com/en/)

**Neighbourhood (Telliskivi / Kalamaja)**
- Telliskivi Creative City: 800+ cultural events a year and **1.5 million+ visitors** (current official site, year not stated) — [Telliskivi – The Loomelinnak story](https://telliskivi.cc/en/about-eng/). Older coverage (c. 2016–2019) said "nearly a million" visitors and 600+ events — [Postimees Kultuur – Telliskivi 10th birthday](https://kultuur.postimees.ee/6559637/telliskivi-loomelinnak-tahistab-oma-10-sunnipaeva-festivaliga); [ERR Eeter](https://eeter.err.ee/932703/telliskivi-loomelinnak-tahistab-sunnipaeva-kumnepaevase-festivaliga)

**Students nearby / in the city**
- Tallinn University (TLÜ) had **7,237 students in 2025** (3,759 bachelor's, 2,775 master's, 316 doctoral; the rest are presumably professional higher education) — [et.wikipedia – Tallinna Ülikool](https://et.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinna_%C3%9Clikool)
- TalTech: "approximately 10,000 students from more than 100 countries" according to one source; another gives about 7,448 (conflicting, years unclear) — [QS TopUniversities – TalTech](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university-technology-taltech); [TalTech – Tule meile](https://taltech.ee/tule-meile)

### Inferences
- The 5.1m figure is probably *visits* (door counts), and many of them are people cutting through between the station, Kalamaja, Telliskivi and the Old Town. That makes it strong for impulse snacking but not a measure of buying intent. Viru Keskus (~15m) and Ülemiste (~7–8m) are bigger; BJT is roughly Rocca al Mare's size. See Q3.
- With 40+ food operators, the street-food floor is crowded with hot-meal competitors. A pie stall would stand out more with a **coffee + slice/hand-pie commuter offer** (morning trains) and **whole pies to take home** (evening commuters) than as another lunch counter.
- The micro-space scheme (1.2 × 1.2 m, 1–30 days) is a **low-cost way to test BJT** before signing a permanent lease. Ask Astri for a test month, ideally including a weekend event.
- Not sourced in this session (verify): Tallinn University's main campus is on Narva mnt (city centre/Kadriorg side), not at Balti jaam. The Estonian Academy of Arts (EKA) is in Kalamaja (Põhja pst), close to BJT. TalTech is in Mustamäe, which links to Balti jaam by bus rather than being next to it. Students at BJT are therefore mostly passers-through (TLÜ/EKA) and train commuters.

## Q2. Old Town (Vanalinn): tourism volumes, seasonality, rents, grab-and-go competitors, restrictions

### Takeaway
Tallinn had about 3.42m foreign visitors in 2025 (mobile-positioning data), but cruise traffic, which feeds the Old Town directly, is only about 190k passengers (2025, +25% y/y) and almost entirely May–October. Current Old Town asking rents in kv.ee listings run from about €11.5 to €28/m²/month, and very small units cost the most per m². Colliers puts prime high-street rents at €18–33/m²/month (Q2 2026). The benchmark grab-and-go seller, III Draakon in the Town Hall, sells pies at €3–3.50. Terraces and street equipment in the Old Town need heritage approval.

### Cited Findings
**Tourism volumes (2025–2026)**

- Tallinn: 254,000 overnight stays in Nov 2025 (+5.8%) and 3.11 million in Jan–Nov 2025 (+3.3%). Bed capacity hit a record 20,327 (+7.9%) and rooms 9,623 (+6.8%) — [Baltic Times – MKM: foreign tourists sustained growth through autumn](https://www.baltictimes.com/mkm__foreign_tourists_sustained_tourism_growth_through_the_autumn_season/) (search-summary attribution)

**Restrictions (heritage, terraces, street trading)**
- Outdoor cafés, small equipment and outdoor advertising in the Old Town (a cultural-heritage area) need a permit. The applicant submits a draft plan with an equipment description in three copies, for the Cultural Heritage Department, the City Centre Government and the entrepreneur — [Tallinn.ee – permits for outdoor cafeterias, small equipment or outside advertisements in Old Town](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/services/granting-permits-installing-outdoor-cafeterias-small-equipment-or-outside-advertisements-tallinn-old)

Minimum fees for commercial use of city property are set by City Government regulation no. 34 of 16 Nov 2022 — [Tallinn.ee – Tänavakaubandus (street trading)](https://www.tallinn.ee/et/tanavakaubandus) (search-summary attribution)

**Seasonal fairs in the Old Town**
- Tallinn Christmas Market 2025 ran 21 Nov – 27 Dec 2025 on Raekoja plats (one source says 28 Dec) — [Puhka Eestis – Tallinna Jõuluturg 2025](https://puhkaeestis.ee/et/tallinna-jouluturg-2111-27122025); [Laadakalender](https://laadakalender.ee/tallinna-jouluturg/)
- City fair stall fees in a 2025 decree of the Tallinn Culture and Sports Department: goods/food vendor in a 3×3 m tent **€48/day**; catering (food truck up to 4×3 m or 3×3 m tent) **€60/day**; student company €10/day. **Caution:** the PDF is hosted on vanalinnapaevad.ee and dated 30.04.2025, so these fees probably apply to the **Old Town Days 2025** fair, not the Christmas market. The search summary linked them to the Christmas market — [Kultuuri- ja Spordiamet decree T-30-1/25/35, annex 1](https://vanalinnapaevad.ee/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/T-30-1_25_35_lisa_1.pdf)
- The city has been considering whether to run the Christmas market itself or tender it out, so the organiser and terms for 2026 may change — [ERR – Tallinn vaeb, kas korraldada jõuluturgu ise või teha hange](https://www.err.ee/1609831014/tallinn-vaeb-kas-korraldada-edaspidi-jouluturgu-ise-voi-teha-hange)

### Inferences
- The Old Town's tourist trade is strongly seasonal. Cruise passengers are zero in Q1, and about 85% arrive in Q2–Q3. A permanent Old Town shop would carry winter rent with thin demand. **Seasonal or event formats** (Old Town Days in June, the Christmas market, a summer pop-up) fit the economics better than a year-round lease.
- Illustrative estimate (not sourced): a 15 m² unit at €425/month base rent equals the gross margin of roughly 200–300 pies a month at €3.30 (about 7–10 a day) at an assumed 45–65% gross margin. Rent is not the main hurdle. Staffing for long tourist-season hours and winter demand are.
- Pirogoff's flagship whole pies (€28 for 900 g on Wolt, see Q5) are not a tourist product. An Old Town outlet would need a **slice/hand-pie range priced at about €3–4** to compete with III Draakon and Maiasmokk.

- Christmas market visitor numbers and actual 2025 stall fees were not found (the fee PDF may refer to Old Town Days).
- Full monthly seasonality of Tallinn tourism (Statistics Estonia TU-tables) was not retrieved.
- The specific heritage rules on shop signage, façade changes and ventilation for food prep in Old Town buildings were not found.

## Q3. Other high-footfall options (malls, transport hubs, campuses, creative quarters, events) and kiosk fit-out costs

### Takeaway
By visitor numbers, Viru Keskus (~15m/yr, next to the Viru bus terminal and Old Town) leads clearly. Next come Ülemiste (~7–8m/yr, where Pirogoff already trades; Rail Baltica terminal due 2028), Kristiine (7.1m, older figure), Rocca al Mare (5.5m, older figure) and BJT (5.1m). Tallinn Airport handled 3.49m passengers and the port 8m+ in 2025. No reliable kiosk fit-out cost data was found for Estonia.

### Cited Findings
**Malls**
- **Viru Keskus**: almost 15 million visitors a year and 30,000+ a day; it calls itself the most efficient shopping centre in the Baltics and Finland. Turnover is about €130m a year (2022: €128m). A multi-year renovation finished in May 2023 (landlord €20m+, tenants €15m). It has 100+ shops and two R-Kiosk units — [Viru Keskus – About](https://virukeskus.com/en/about-viru-keskus); [Kapitel – Viru Keskus](https://kapitel.ee/retail/viru-keskus/); [Viru Keskus – R Kiosk I korrus](https://virukeskus.com/en/shops/r-kiosk-i-korrus)
- **Ülemiste keskus**: about 20,000 visitors a day on average and about 660,000 a month (≈7.3–7.9m a year, derived), with ~120 million visits since opening (20 years, 2004–2024). 2024 was its best year on record. The following year (2025, per the centre director's interview) visitors and revenue fell 3–4% and the centre operated at a loss — [Ülemiste – KKK](https://www.ulemiste.ee/kkk/); [Ülemiste keskus sai 20](https://www.ulemiste.ee/ulemiste-keskus-sai-20-avapaevast-alates-on-keskust-kulastanud-120-miljonit-inimest/); [Ülemiste keskuse juht: läheb raskemaks](https://www.ulemiste.ee/ulemiste-keskuse-juht-laheb-raskemaks-aga-investeeringud-piirkonda-peavad-jatkuma/)
- Ülemiste area: about €500m of investment expected in the district. Ülemiste City business turnover was ~€3bn in 2024 — [ERR – Ülemiste piirkonda ootab ees 500 miljoni mahus investeeringuid](https://www.err.ee/1609931549/ulemiste-piirkonda-ootab-ees-500-miljoni-mahus-investeeringuid)
- **Kristiine Keskus**: 7.1 million visitors a year (**dated figure, c. 2013–2019**) — [Wikipedia – Kristiine Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristiine_Centre)
- **Rocca al Mare**: 5.5 million visitors a year (**dated, c. 2015**) — [Kaubandus.ee – Rocca al Mare tahab saada suurimaks keskuseks (2015)](https://www.kaubandus.ee/uudised/2015/06/18/rocca-al-mare-tahab-saada-eesti-suurimaks-keskuseks)
- **T1 Mall of Tallinn**: opened 2018, ~55,000–57,000 m², 200+ stores, bought by Lintgen for €55m. No footfall figure found. In 2026 its Cinamon IMAX cinema closed (April) and Estonia's first HalfPrice store opened there (June), showing a shift to value retail — [Wikipedia – T1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T1_Mall_of_Tallinn); [Ellex – Lintgen acquires T1](https://ellex.legal/project/lintgen-acquires-t1-mall-of-tallinn/); [Colliers Q2 2026](https://www.colliers.com/en-lv/countries/latvia/reports/2026/q2-2026-baltic-property-snapshot)
- **Solaris**: Pirogoff has (or had) a Solaris venue on Wolt (Estonia pst 9). No Solaris footfall data found — [Wolt – Pirogoff Solaris](https://wolt.com/en/est/tallinn/venue/pirogoff-solaris)

**Transport hubs**
- **Ülemiste rail / Rail Baltica**: the first section of the Ülemiste "Linda" terminal (a new platform) opened to passengers in June 2025. Full construction begins in 2026 and the terminal is due in 2028. It will link 1520 mm and 1435 mm rail, trams, a six-bay bus terminal and walking/cycling routes — [ERR – First Rail Baltica platform opens](https://news.err.ee/1609720263/first-rail-baltica-platform-opens-in-tallinn-as-full-build-to-begin-in-2026); [Rail Baltica – first section completed](https://www.railbaltica.org/news/first-section-of-ulemiste-linda-terminal-completed-passengers-now-using-the-new-platform/); [Parametric Architecture – ZHA Ülemiste terminal opens 2028](https://parametric-architecture.com/ulemiste-terminal-by-zha-begins-construction-opens-2028/)
- **Tallinn Airport**: 3,488,973 passengers in 2025 (−0.1% y/y). All Estonian airports together served a record 3,582,489 — [Tallinn Airport – 2025 results](https://airport.ee/en/tallinn-airports-2025-financial-results-strong-profitability-and-remarkable-revenue-growth/)
- **Port of Tallinn (incl. D-terminal / Helsinki ferries)**: just over 8 million passengers in 2025 (+1.0%). No D-terminal split was found — [Inderes – Tallinna Sadam FY2025](https://www.inderes.fi/en/releases/as-tallinna-sadam-operational-volumes-for-2025-full-year-and-q4)
- **City transport**: ~130m passengers in 2024; ~20m tram journeys a year (see Q1) — [Tallinn.ee](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/nearly-130-million-passengers-used-tallinns-public-transport-2024)

## Q7. B2C viability: own shops vs dark kitchen + delivery + pick-up points; margins and break-even for small kiosks

### Takeaway
No public data on margins or break-even for small Tallinn food kiosks was found. The indirect evidence points to caution about adding fixed-cost shops. Mall trade is under pressure (Ülemiste −3–4% and loss-making in 2025; Colliers says retailers are asking for rent cuts). Prime rents are sticky (€23–45/m²), platform commissions are 20–30%, and tourist demand is seasonal. A hybrid model fits this evidence best: one high-footfall flagship or kiosk, plus pickup and delivery from the production kitchen, plus seasonal pop-ups.

### Cited Findings
- Retail conditions: Colliers Q3 2025 said the outlook for retailers and landlords would stay difficult through end-2025, with many retailers optimising or seeking rent reductions. Vacancy is 2.2–2.3%, so landlords hold firm on rents of €23–45/m²/month — [Colliers Q3 2025](https://www.colliers.com/en-lv/countries/latvia/reports/q3-2025-baltic-property-snapshot); [Colliers Q2 2026](https://www.colliers.com/en-lv/countries/latvia/reports/2026/q2-2026-baltic-property-snapshot)
- Mall traffic trend: Ülemiste had a record 2024, then visitors and revenue fell 3–4% with an operating loss — [Ülemiste keskuse juht: läheb raskemaks](https://www.ulemiste.ee/ulemiste-keskuse-juht-laheb-raskemaks-aga-investeeringud-piirkonda-peavad-jatkuma/)
- Delivery economics: commission of 20% (pickup) to 25–30% (delivery), and venues co-fund free-delivery campaigns — [ERR 972390](https://www.err.ee/972390/tasuta-lounaid-pole-olemas-restoranid-maksavad-toidu-kullerveo-eest-rasvaseid-tasusid); [ERR 1069813](https://www.err.ee/1069813/wolti-tasuta-kojuveo-kulust-poole-peavad-tasuma-toidukohad)
- Low-commitment physical formats exist: BJT micro-spaces for 1–30 days ([Astri Zendesk](https://astri.zendesk.com/hc/et/articles/115003293485-Mikropinnad-Balti-Jaama-Turul)); city fair pitches at €48–60 a day ([2025 decree PDF](https://vanalinnapaevad.ee/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/T-30-1_25_35_lisa_1.pdf)); small Old Town units from €425 a month ([kv.ee Rüütli](https://www.kv.ee/valja-uurida-multifunktsionaalse-kasutusalaga-arip-3033606.html))
- Tourism is still growing (3.42m foreign visitors in 2025; cruise +25%), but accommodated tourists in Estonia remain 3% below 2019 — [Tallinn.ee](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies); [Statistics Estonia](https://stat.ee/en/news/number-accommodated-tourists-increased-little-2025)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative break-even framework (estimate; all inputs are assumptions except rent, fees and prices cited above):**
  - A 10 m² mall kiosk at €35/m² is about €350 a month base rent, plus unknown service charges. Staffing 11–12 h a day × 7 days ≈ 2 FTE, the dominant cost (Estonian wage data not collected here).
  - At €3.50 a slice/hand-pie and an assumed 55–65% gross margin (about €2.00 contribution per unit), each €1,000 of fixed monthly cost needs about 500 extra units a month (about 17 a day).
  - The same unit sold via Wolt delivery at 25% commission gives about €1.10–1.40 contribution, so delivery of single pieces is marginal. A €28 whole pie at 25% commission still contributes roughly €8–11 at the same assumed margin.
- On this logic, shops pay off mainly as **brand/pickup hubs** that also feed click-and-collect (20% commission) and whole-pie orders. A dark-kitchen-only model gives up the impulse slice trade that the client's "walk-around portions" idea targets.
