### Inferences
- The legal address (a flat number in Ilmandu village, Harku parish) looks residential, so it is likely the owner's home rather than a production site. The bakery activity runs out of the Tiskre Prisma unit (see section 3).
- Julia Pak probably handles bookkeeping and HR contact for Pirogoff: she is the job-ad contact, her accounting firm WaveReports is linked to Nuvira's e-mail, and she shares the owner's surname. A family relationship is likely but **unverified**.
- Nuvira Foods (wholesale, "foods") may be a vehicle for a new wholesale/B2B food line or a pivot with a partner holding the majority. It had no trading through Q2 2026. This matters for a turnaround plan: the owner may already be diverting attention or assets to a new venture.
- This is a key-person business. One owner and director, no supervisory board, no outside investors.

## 2. Financials, taxes, employees, debts and legal proceedings

### Takeaway
Pirogoff is a **~€130k/year micro-business in slow decline**. Revenue jumped from €59k (2021, first trading year) to a peak of €168k (2022), slid to €133k (2024) and €132k (2025), and is running about 18% below last year in H1 2026 (taxable turnover €57.1k vs €69.4k). Profit has never exceeded €11.5k. 2025 closed with a **€7.2k loss** and equity fell to €9.1k. There are no tax debts, no court cases and no insolvency. The only legal matters are a 2024 registry penalty warning for a late annual report and two brief tax-debt episodes in 2026.

### Inferences
- Lifecycle: the company was a dormant shell in 2019–2020 (revenue €55 and €0). Trading began in Q2–Q3 2021, which matches the Facebook page being created on 13 Jun 2021 and the first shops (Ülemiste, Haabersti Rimi) operating by Nov 2021. Revenue peaked in Q4 2022 at €49.8k for the quarter. Since then quarterly turnover has drifted from about €40–46k (2023) to about €33–35k (2024–Q3 2025) and about €27–30k (H1 2026). The trailing four quarters to Q2 2026 total about €122.9k.
- The 2024 profit (€11.5k) came almost entirely from cutting payroll. Labour taxes fell from about €13k (2023) to about €4.8k (2024), with only 1 registered employee for most of the year. When staff rose to 3–4 in 2025 (labour taxes +45%) revenue did not grow, and the company swung to a loss. The core problem is volume per outlet, not cost.
- Registered staffing looks thin for 3–4 outlets plus baking, with 1–4 registered employees and estimated average gross wages of €520–910/month (below Estonia's minimum wage, which suggests part-time contracts). The owner probably works unpaid in the business. The Töötukassa payments (€4,116) are probably wage subsidies for hiring registered unemployed people (unverified).
- Unit-economics sketch (inference): 2025 revenue of €132k divided by an average pie price of about €27 incl. VAT (about €22 ex-VAT) gives roughly 6,000 pies a year, or about 16–17 pies a day across all outlets. That is about 4 pies per outlet per day if 4 outlets operate on about 5 days a week. With 4 outlets, revenue is about €33k per outlet per year (about €2.8k per month), likely below the cost of a staffed kiosk once rent and wages are included.
- The balance sheet is shrinking. Equity fell to €9.1k by end-2025 and was projected at about €7.3k for 2026, and current liabilities of about €14k leave little buffer. Small tax arrears in 2026 (€360) hint at tight cash. The company is not yet near the Estonian net-assets-below-half-of-share-capital threshold (½ × €2,500 = €1,250), but continued losses at the 2025 rate would erode equity within about 1–2 years.

### Gaps

- Rent levels, lease terms with Rimi/Prisma, and any owner loans are unknown.
