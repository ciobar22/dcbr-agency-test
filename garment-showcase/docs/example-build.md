# Two prints to a working garment showcase

This example starts from two graphics supplied by Amir, generated by him in Midjourney and ChatGPT. It does not use catalog photography from a clothing brand.

## Inputs and image anchors

The first concept is a white oversized cotton T-shirt with a full-color alpine illustration on its chest. The second is the same cut in pastel pink, with black line-art cards on its chest. Both backs are plain.

Generate a blank front photograph of the garment on a dark wooden hanger against a uniform green background. Use the exact artwork file as the print source. If the image model reinterprets the artwork, composite the original graphic onto the blank base, carrying subtle local fabric shading into the ink. Remove the paper background from black line art rather than placing a white square on colored cotton.

Derive a strict 90-degree profile from the accepted front. Match the canvas, wooden apex, object height and lighting. Keep the hook and rail outside these images. Generate a plain rear view for the detail card.

Normalize the two video anchors to the same 1920×1080 canvas, with a shared suspension point and equal garment height.

## Video generation

The white T-shirt was generated with LTX-2.5 Pro: 6 seconds, 1080p, 50 fps, silent, profile as first frame and front as last frame. Two paid takes were produced. The selected take was processed further to stabilize the suspension point and the hanger color.

The pink T-shirt's first LTX take exposed its back before revealing the print. It was rejected. A second LTX request was rejected for insufficient balance before generation. The replacement used Kling 3.0 through Pika: 5 seconds, 1080p, silent, the same endpoints, one shot.

The prompt must state that the printed front becomes visible immediately, the back never appears, and the movement is exactly one quarter-turn. The camera, light, garment scale and upper wooden apex stay fixed. No swinging, rocking, full spin or return.

Actual generation spend: three LTX takes at $1.02 each, plus one Kling take at $0.45: **$3.51**. Prices were checked on the providers' live documentation/catalog on 14 October 2026. They can change.

## Processing and interface

Selected useful footage: white 0–4.2 seconds; pink 1.0–4.6 seconds, removing its initial wait. Flat green was keyed, preserving the dark green ink in the landscape. Light-fabric spill was reduced. The hanger tone and suspension apex were stabilized. Thirty selected frames per garment were packed into transparent WebP atlases.

The hold images are decoded directly from the atlas endpoints. This prevents a visible swap between an animated endpoint and an unrelated PNG. A stationary wire is drawn by the interface.

The browser needs no generation account. It drives a short reversible playhead: 80ms hover intent, 360ms turn, 280ms return, with endpoint easing. Neighbor opacity stays at 1. On mobile, the centered item turns after the rail settles; tapping opens the detail view.

Review footage at actual UI speed. A visually pleasant full-length clip can still expose swing, lighting changes or artwork deformation when compressed into a short interaction. Generated concepts and footage can alter fine details; the source artwork remains the reference for fidelity.
